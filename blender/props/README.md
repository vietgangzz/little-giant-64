# Little Giant 64 — world props

Every prop in `assets/models/props/*.glb` is generated by Python here. Nothing is modelled by
hand and nothing uses image textures: each surface is a role material (`M_Gold`, `M_Leaf`, …) with
a base colour and roughness, and the detail (bevels, rims, grooves, the drum-face reliefs) is
geometry.

## Rebuild

```sh
# everything: props.blend, all .glb files, then the labelled contact sheets
blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 -P blender/props/build_props.py -- --render

# iterate on a few props (does not overwrite props.blend unless --save)
blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 -P blender/props/build_props.py -- --only star,coin --render
```

Options after `--`: `--only a,b`, `--render`, `--no-export`, `--no-save`, `--save`.
`PROPS_DEBUG_TRIS=1` prints the triangle count of every part.

Outputs:

- `blender/props/props.blend`: one collection per prop, laid out in a row along +X.
- `assets/models/props/<name>.glb`: glTF binary, +Y up, modifiers applied, no cameras or lights.
- `blender/props/renders/sheet_*.png`: EEVEE contact sheets, 4 × 3 tiles, 1600 px wide. Per-prop tiles
  are in `renders/tiles/`.

A full build takes about 6 s, and rendering takes about 15 s more.

## Checkpoint flag (shader route)

The cloth is a separate mesh object named **`Flag`**, parented to the pole. It is a 16 × 10 grid, 1.0 × 0.64 m,
with 1.6 cm of thickness. It has two surfaces: `M_Lime` and `M_EyeGreen` (the brand gang eyes
from `blender/common/mascot_artwork.json`, printed on both sides).

The `Flag` origin sits on the pole edge at the top of the cloth, so local X runs from 0 (pole) to
1.0 (free end). `shaders/flag.gdshader` uses that directly: `pole_x = 0` and
`cloth_length = 1.0`. The flag also has UVs, with `UV.x` running from 0 at the pole to 1 at the
free end.

There are no flag bones.

## Crab rig

The mesh is `crab`, rigidly skinned. Every part is weighted 100 % to one bone. The armature is named
`Armature`.

- The bones are `body`, `claw.L`, `claw.R`, `leg.L.1..3`, `leg.R.1..3`, `eye.L` and `eye.R`. There are also two
  extras: `pincer.L` and `pincer.R` are the movable upper fingers, children of the claws.
- `.L` is the crab's left, which is +X in Blender (the crab faces −Y).
- The `body` head sits on the ground, so a body scale on bone Y flattens the whole crab onto the floor.
- The actions are exported at 30 fps:
  - `walk`: 0.6 s, tripod gait, sideways sway. It is authored to loop.
  - `snap`: 0.5 s, claws up, snap twice.
  - `squash`: 0.4 s, and it ends flattened.

  glTF has no loop flag, so set `walk` to `Animation.LOOP_LINEAR` in Godot.

## Gotchas

- **Blender 5.x API.** `Action.fcurves` is gone. Actions are keyed with `pose_bone.keyframe_insert`, then
  each one is pushed to its own NLA track, which the exporter picks up in `ACTIONS` mode.
  The strips sit at frames 0, 40 and 80 with extrapolation `NOTHING`. Frame 30 therefore shows the rest
  pose in `props.blend`.
- **Winding.** Every prop is a closed shell and is exported single-sided (backface culling is on). `join_parts` warns when a
  closed part has a negative signed volume, which means inward normals. When you write a new lathe profile, keep the outside on
  the right of the direction of travel in the (r, z) plane: bottom → outer wall up → top inward.
- **Matrices with a mirror** (det < 0) flip faces. Use rotations, as `face_frame` does, not axis swaps.
- **Shading** comes from per-part auto-smooth angles, written as sharp edges. There are no custom normals.
  The faceted parts (star points, drum stars, birds) are flat-shaded on purpose.
- **Tilted trunks.** Tilted trunk and stem caps are flattened onto z = 0 after the build (`settle_on_ground`),
  so the lowest point of each prop is its origin height.
- **`?` glyph.** The "?" is built from geometry (`motifs.question_mark`), so the build does not depend on a system font.
- **Cloud.** The cloud is a union of spheres, voxel-remeshed, smoothed and then decimated to about 900 triangles.
- **Godot materials.** Godot's `Fx.toonify` reads `albedo_color` and the material name. `Gold` and `Bronze` names get
  a metallic toon look, and `Eye` names skip the ink outline. Keep the `M_<Role>` names when you
  add parts.
